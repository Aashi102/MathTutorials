Algebra Word Problems

Word problems connect algebra to real‑life situations. The key is translating words into equations.

Step 1: Identify the Unknown

Decide what the variable represents. This is the most important step.

Example: “Sarah has 5 more apples than Tom.”

Let x = Tom’s apples → Sarah = x + 5

Step 2: Write an Equation

Use the relationships in the problem to form an equation.

Example: “Together they have 17 apples.”

x + (x + 5) = 17

Step 3: Solve & Interpret

Solve the equation, then interpret your answer in context.

Example: Solve the equation above.

x = 6 → Tom has 6 apples, Sarah has 11.

Practice Problems

  • A number increased by 7 is 19.
  • Twice a number minus 3 is 11.
  • A rectangle has perimeter 30. Length is x, width is x − 3.
  • A taxi charges $4 plus $2 per mile. Write the cost function.
