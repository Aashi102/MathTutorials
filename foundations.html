Algebra Foundations

Before you can solve equations or graph lines, you need to understand the basic building blocks of algebra: variables, expressions, and the order of operations.

What Is Algebra?

Algebra is the language of patterns and relationships. It lets us describe how quantities change and how they relate to each other. Instead of working with specific numbers, we work with symbols that can represent many possible values.

Example:

If a movie ticket costs $x and you buy 3 tickets, the total cost is 3x. This works whether the ticket costs $5, $10, or $12.50.

Variables

A variable is a letter that represents a number. It allows us to write general rules instead of specific cases.

Example: In the expression x + 5, the variable is x.

Variables make algebra flexible. Instead of solving one problem, you solve a whole family of problems at once.

Order of Operations

To avoid confusion, mathematicians agreed on a standard order for evaluating expressions. You may know it as PEMDAS:

  • Parentheses
  • Exponents
  • Multiplication & Division
  • Addition & Subtraction

Example: Evaluate 3 + 2 × 4

Multiply first: 2 × 4 = 8 → then add: 3 + 8 = 11.
