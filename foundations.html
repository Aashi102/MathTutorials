The Real Number System Taxonomy

Numbers are categorized into nested sets based on their mathematical properties. Recognizing which set a number belongs to allows us to understand what operational rules apply.

Natural Numbers ($\mathbb{N}$)

The counting numbers starting from $1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$ Used exclusively for discrete counting tasks.

Whole Numbers ($\mathbb{W}$)

All natural numbers plus zero: $0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$ Essential for representing empty states.

Integers ($\mathbb{Z}$)

Whole numbers and their negative opposites: $\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$

Rational Numbers ($\mathbb{Q}$)

Any number that can be expressed as a fraction $\frac{p}{q}$ where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $q \neq 0$. Examples: $\frac{3}{4}, -2.5, 0.\bar{3}$.

Irrational Numbers ($\mathbb{I}$)

Numbers that cannot be written as fractions. Their decimals run infinitely without repeating: $\pi, e, \sqrt{2}$.

Real Numbers ($\mathbb{R}$)

The complete union of rational and irrational number sets. Every point on a standard line graph is a real number.

Order of Operations (PEMDAS / BODMAS)

To eliminate ambiguity in multi-step numerical calculations, mathematicians established a universal hierarchy. Without this agreed-upon sequence, an expression like $6 + 2 \times 4$ could produce either $32$ or $14$.

The Operational Hierarchy Breakdown

  1. P / B — Parentheses & Brackets: Resolve grouping symbols first from the inside out: $( ), [ ], \{ \}$. Fractional numerators and denominators act as implicit grouping brackets!
  2. E / O — Exponents & Orders: Evaluate powers, roots, and absolute value magnitudes next (e.g., $3^3, \sqrt{16}, |-5|$).
  3. M & D — Multiplication & Division: These share equal precedence. Execute them strictly from left to right as they appear in the sequence.
  4. A & S — Addition & Subtraction: These also share equal precedence. Execute them strictly from left to right as they appear in the sequence.

Guided Example 1 (Standard Operations): Simplify $18 \div 3 \times 2 + (5 - 2)^2$

Step 1 (Parentheses): $(5 - 2) = 3 \implies 18 \div 3 \times 2 + 3^2$

Step 2 (Exponents): $3^2 = 9 \implies 18 \div 3 \times 2 + 9$

Step 3 (Division & Multiplication Left-to-Right):

First operation left-to-right is division: $18 \div 3 = 6 \implies 6 \times 2 + 9$

Next operation is multiplication: $6 \times 2 = 12 \implies 12 + 9$

Step 4 (Addition): $12 + 9 = \mathbf{21}$

Guided Example 2 (Fractional Grouping Pitfall): Evaluate $\frac{4^2 + 8}{2 \times (7 - 4)}$

Note: Treat numerator and denominator as separate bracketed expressions: $\frac{(4^2 + 8)}{(2 \times (7 - 4))}$

Step 1 (Numerator Exponent): $4^2 = 16 \implies \text{Numerator} = 16 + 8 = 24$

Step 2 (Denominator Parentheses): $(7 - 4) = 3 \implies \text{Denominator} = 2 \times 3 = 6$

Step 3 (Final Fraction Division): $\frac{24}{6} = \mathbf{4}$

Mastering Operations with Signed Numbers

Mismanaging positive and negative signs is one of the most common causes of arithmetic errors in algebra. Master these core mechanics until they become second nature.

Addition & Subtraction

Same Signs: Add the absolute values together and keep the common sign.
Example: $-5 + (-8) = -13$

Different Signs: Subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger magnitude. Assign the sign of the larger magnitude number.
Example: $-12 + 7 = -5$

Double Negatives: Subtracting a negative is mathematically identical to adding a positive: $a - (-b) = a + b$.
Example: $4 - (-9) = 4 + 9 = 13$

Multiplication & Division

Like Signs Yield Positive:
$(+) \times (+) = (+)$
$(-) \times (-) = (+)$
Example: $(-6) \times (-4) = +24$

Unlike Signs Yield Negative:
$(+) \times (-) = (-)$
$(-) \times (+) = (-)$
Example: $(-20) \div 5 = -4$

Fundamental Field Axioms of Algebra

Every algebraic transformation—whether simplifying terms or expanding equations—is legally governed by fundamental properties of real numbers.

Property Name Addition Rule Multiplication Rule
Commutative $a + b = b + a$ $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$
Associative $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$ $(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$
Identity Element $a + 0 = a$ (Additive Identity: 0) $a \cdot 1 = a$ (Multiplicative Identity: 1)
Inverse Element $a + (-a) = 0$ $a \cdot \frac{1}{a} = 1$ (where $a \neq 0$)
Distributive $a(b + c) = ab + ac$  |  Multiplies across addition/subtraction
