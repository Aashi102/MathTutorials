Algebra Foundations

Build the language and core ideas that everything else depends on.

What is Algebra?

Algebra is a way of using symbols—usually letters—to represent numbers and relationships. Instead of working with only specific numbers, algebra lets us describe patterns and rules.

Key Building Blocks

Variables

A variable is a symbol (often a letter like \( x \) or \( y \)) that stands for a number we don’t know yet or that can change.

Example: In \( 2x + 3 \), the letter \( x \) is the variable.

Constants

A constant is a fixed value—it doesn’t change in a given problem.

Example: In \( 2x + 3 \), the number \( 3 \) is a constant.

Expressions

An expression is a combination of variables, numbers, and operations (like +, −, ×, ÷) but it does not have an equals sign.

Example: \( 4x - 7 \) is an expression.

Equations

An equation says that two expressions are equal. It always has an equals sign.

Example: \( 4x - 7 = 9 \) is an equation.

Evaluating Expressions

To evaluate an expression means to find its value when you know the value of the variable.

If \( x = 5 \), then \( 2x + 3 = 2(5) + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13 \).

Try It Mentally

Suppose \( x = 4 \). What is the value of \( 3x - 2 \)?

Hint: Multiply 3 by 4, then subtract 2.

\( 3x - 2 = 3(4) - 2 = 12 - 2 = 10 \).

Combining Like Terms

Terms are “like” if they have the same variable part. You can combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients.

\( 2x + 5x = 7x \) because both terms are “x” terms.

Simplify \( 3x + 4 + 2x - 1 \).

Combine the \( x \)-terms: \( 3x + 2x = 5x \). Combine the constants: \( 4 - 1 = 3 \). Final answer: \( 5x + 3 \).

Foundations Practice

Try these problems to solidify the basics.

  1. Identify the variable and constant in \( 7x + 2 \).
  2. Evaluate \( 4x + 1 \) when \( x = 3 \).
  3. Simplify \( 2x + 3x - 4 \).
  4. Write an equation to represent: “Five more than a number is 12.”
