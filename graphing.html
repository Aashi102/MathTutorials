Understanding Graphing

Graphing turns algebra into pictures. It helps you visualize relationships, patterns, and how quantities change.

The Coordinate Plane

The coordinate plane is made of two perpendicular number lines: the x‑axis (horizontal) and the y‑axis (vertical).

Example: Plot the point (3, −2)

Move 3 units right (positive x), then 2 units down (negative y).

Slope

Slope measures steepness. It tells you how much a line rises or falls as you move to the right.

Formula: slope = rise / run

Example: Find slope between (1, 2) and (3, 6)

Rise = 6 − 2 = 4 Run = 3 − 1 = 2 Slope = 4/2 = 2

Slope‑Intercept Form

Many lines can be written as:

y = mx + b

  • m = slope
  • b = y‑intercept

Example: Graph y = 2x + 1

Start at (0, 1). Use slope 2 → up 2, right 1. Plot several points and connect them.
