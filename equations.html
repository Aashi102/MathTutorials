Mastering Equations

Equations are the foundation of algebra — they’re like puzzles waiting to be solved. Each equation tells a story of balance: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. Understanding this balance helps you find the value of the variable that makes the statement true.

In this lesson, you’ll learn how to solve equations step‑by‑step, from simple one‑step problems to multi‑step and complex equations involving parentheses, fractions, and decimals.

What Is an Equation?

An equation is a mathematical statement that shows two expressions are equal. It always includes an equals sign (=). Think of it like a balance scale — both sides must weigh the same.

The goal of solving an equation is to find the value of the variable (like x or y) that keeps both sides balanced.

Examples:

  • x + 5 = 12 → both sides are equal when x = 7
  • 3y − 2 = 10 → both sides are equal when y = 4
  • 2(a + 1) = 14 → both sides are equal when a = 6

One‑Step Equations

A one‑step equation can be solved in a single move. You use the inverse operation — the opposite of what’s happening — to isolate the variable. If the equation adds something, subtract it. If it multiplies, divide.

Example 1 (Easy): Solve x + 7 = 12

Subtract 7 from both sides → x = 5.

Example 2 (Medium): Solve 5x = 20

Divide both sides by 5 → x = 4.

Example 3 (Complex): Solve x / 8 = 6

Multiply both sides by 8 → x = 48.

Two‑Step Equations

Two‑step equations require two moves to isolate the variable. First undo addition or subtraction, then undo multiplication or division. Always work backward from the operations closest to the variable.

Example 1 (Easy): Solve 2x − 4 = 10

Add 4 to both sides → 2x = 14 → divide by 2 → x = 7.

Example 2 (Medium): Solve 3x + 2 = 17

Subtract 2 → 3x = 15 → divide by 3 → x = 5.

Example 3 (Complex): Solve 4x / 2 − 3 = 5

Add 3 → 4x / 2 = 8 → multiply both sides by 2 → 4x = 16 → divide by 4 → x = 4.

Multi‑Step Equations

Multi‑step equations combine several operations — parentheses, distribution, and combining like terms. Simplify each side first, then isolate the variable step by step. The key is patience and keeping both sides balanced.

Example 1 (Easy): Solve 2(x + 3) = 14

Distribute → 2x + 6 = 14 → subtract 6 → 2x = 8 → divide by 2 → x = 4.

Example 2 (Medium): Solve 3x + 4 = 2x + 9

Subtract 2x from both sides → x + 4 = 9 → subtract 4 → x = 5.

Example 3 (Complex): Solve 4(x − 2) + 3 = 19

Distribute → 4x − 8 + 3 = 19 → combine like terms → 4x − 5 = 19 → add 5 → 4x = 24 → divide by 4 → x = 6.
