Algebraic Expressions

An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase combining variables, numbers, exponents, and operational symbols without an equal sign ($=$). Expressions represent quantities that vary or remain unknown.

In this comprehensive module, you will learn to dissect expression anatomy, apply the distributive property, collect like terms efficiently, evaluate multi-variable terms, and translate complex verbal phrases into formal mathematical syntax.

Anatomy of an Algebraic Expression

Before performing algebraic operations, we need precise language to identify components within an expression.

8x³ − 5xy + 2y − 11

Terms

Individual structural chunks separated by $+$ or $-$ operators. The terms above are $8x^3$, $-5xy$, $2y$, and $-11$.

Coefficients

The numerical factor multiplying a variable. In $8x^3$, the coefficient is $8$. In $-5xy$, it is $-5$.

Variables & Exponents

Letters representing unknowns ($x, y$). Exponents ($3$) specify how many times a base variable multiplies itself.

Constants

A fixed numerical quantity with no variable factors attached. In this expression, $-11$ is the constant.

Collecting & Combining Like Terms

Like terms are terms that possess identical variable bases raised to identical exponential powers. Only the coefficients vary. Terms with differing variables or exponent levels (like $x$ and $x^2$) cannot be added or subtracted together.

The Golden Rule of Combining: Add or subtract only the numerical coefficients while keeping the shared variable factors and exponents unchanged:
$$ax^n + bx^n = (a + b)x^n$$

Guided Example 1: Simplify $6x^2 + 4x - 2x^2 + 9 - 7x - 3$

Step 1 (Identify and group like terms):

• Group $x^2$ terms: $6x^2 - 2x^2$

• Group linear $x$ terms: $4x - 7x$

• Group constant terms: $9 - 3$

Step 2 (Combine coefficients):

• $x^2$ terms: $(6 - 2)x^2 = 4x^2$

• $x$ terms: $(4 - 7)x = -3x$

• Constants: $9 - 3 = 6$

Final Simplified Expression: $\mathbf{4x^2 - 3x + 6}$

The Distributive Property & Expanding Parentheses

The distributive property allows us to clear parentheses by multiplying an outer term across every inner term within a grouped sum or difference: $$a(b + c) = ab + ac \quad \text{and} \quad a(b - c) = ab - ac$$

Critical Pitfall: Distributing Negative Signs!

When a negative sign or negative coefficient sits outside parentheses, that negative sign must flip the sign of every single term inside during expansion: $$-(3x - 7) = -3x + 7$$

Guided Example 2: Expand and simplify $3(4x - 2) - 2(x - 5)$

Step 1 (Distribute 3 into first binomial): $3 \times 4x - 3 \times 2 = 12x - 6$

Step 2 (Distribute -2 into second binomial): $-2 \times x - (-2) \times 5 = -2x + 10$

Step 3 (Reassemble expanded terms): $12x - 6 - 2x + 10$

Step 4 (Collect like terms): $(12x - 2x) + (-6 + 10) = \mathbf{10x + 4}$

Evaluating Expressions via Substitution

Evaluating an algebraic expression means replacing its variables with specific numerical values, then applying standard PEMDAS rules to calculate a final value.

Guided Example 3: Evaluate $2x^2 - 3xy + y^2$ for $x = -3$ and $y = 4$

Step 1 (Substitute with safety parentheses):

$2(-3)^2 - 3(-3)(4) + (4)^2$

Step 2 (Compute all exponents):

$(-3)^2 = 9$ and $4^2 = 16 \implies 2(9) - 3(-3)(4) + 16$

Step 3 (Compute all multiplications):

$2(9) = 18$ and $-3(-3)(4) = +36 \implies 18 + 36 + 16$

Step 4 (Add values):

$18 + 36 + 16 = \mathbf{70}$
