Algebraic Expressions

Expressions are the building blocks of algebra. They represent quantities that may change and allow us to describe patterns, relationships, and rules.

What Is an Expression?

An expression is a combination of numbers, variables, and operations. Unlike equations, expressions do not have an equals sign.

Examples:

  • 3x + 5
  • 7 − y
  • 2(a + 4)

Combining Like Terms

Terms are “like” if they have the same variable part. You can combine them by adding or subtracting their coefficients.

Example: Simplify 3x + 5x

3x + 5x = 8x

More examples:

  • 4y − 2y = 2y
  • 7a + 3a = 10a
  • 9m − m = 8m

Evaluating Expressions

To evaluate an expression, substitute a value for the variable and simplify.

Example: Evaluate 2x + 3 when x = 4

2(4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11

Try these:

  • Evaluate 5x − 2 when x = 3
  • Evaluate 3(a + 1) when a = 7
  • Evaluate 10 − y when y = 6

Practice Problems

  • Simplify: 6x + 2x
  • Simplify: 9y − 4y
  • Evaluate: 3x + 1 when x = 5
  • Evaluate: 2(a − 3) when a = 10
